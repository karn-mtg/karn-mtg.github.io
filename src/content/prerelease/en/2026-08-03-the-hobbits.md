generalInfoIntro: 'The Hobbits is a lean 193-card set with unusually even color balance — every color sits at 29-31 cards, tighter than most sets. Multicolor (31 cards, 24 of them creatures) and Red (31 cards, 20 creatures) are the most creature-heavy slots, while Colorless is small at just 13 cards.'
manaCurveIntro: 'Weighing each two-color pair against a real 6-pack pool (not just full-set totals, which every pair clears easily) against a rough 40-card, 15-17 creature curve target, Blue-Green comes out on top at 95.6% expected coverage, with Blue-Red (88.8%) and Black-Green (86.9%) close behind — all three combine a deep 2-drop slot with enough top-end to not run dry past turn 5. White-Black is the clear laggard at 68.8%, thin at exactly the slots that matter most: only 1.4 expected 4-drops and 0.3 expected cards at 6+, meaning that pair is the one most likely to have real curve gaps in an actual sealed pool rather than just on paper.'
synergyIntro: 'Real synergy in this set mostly spans two different cards, not one — a payoff on one card, and separate, cheaper cards that generate what it wants. Graveyard/Mill Matters is the pairing most likely to actually come together in a sealed pool (comfortably the top combo score), with Draw Matters close behind; Sacrifice Matters and +1/+1 Counters Matter barely register, since the set prints plenty of counter-granting effects but almost nothing that specifically pays off a counter landing. A handful of cards pull double or triple duty across these themes at once — The Sackville-Bagginses and Belladonna Took each touch four different synergy themes — which makes them worth grabbing on sight even outside their raw B.R.E.A.D. score.'
breadIntro: 'Every card ran through the same rules-based B.R.E.A.D. scorer used across our pre-release coverage.'
analysisIntro: 'WotC names five two-color archetypes for this set. Weighing each against a real 6-pack sealed pool, only Red-White Dwarves (Storied) comes back as genuinely well supported end to end. The most striking gap: Black-Green Wolves is our own #1-ranked color pair by raw power, but its named Ferocious payoffs are so thin — six cards total, none rare or mythic — that the archetype itself rates as insufficiently supported. Our data flags Trample as the stronger, better-supported build-around in those same colors instead — the pair is strong, it''s just not the "Wolves" plan WotC describes.'
introText: 'The Hobbits is Magic''s next Universes Beyond trip through Middle-earth, releasing August 14, 2026 as a small, Play-Booster-only set built around Bilbo''s journey — new mechanics Storied and Recruit, hone counters, and the return of Amass (now building Goblin armies) and Adventure cards. WotC ships five two-color draft archetypes for it: Humans (Recruit), Goblins (Amass), Wolves (Ferocious), Dwarves (Storied), and Elves (Landfall). Here''s what actually holds up once you weigh those plans against a real 6-pack sealed pool.'
wordCloudIntro: 'Draw is the single most common word across this set''s oracle text by a wide margin, with counter, enters, and target close behind — a set that rewards both card selection and stapling triggered abilities onto creatures entering or attacking. All four sit at or near a 100% pull chance across a 6-pack pool, so you will see this vocabulary in nearly every sealed deck regardless of colors.'
kindredIntro: 'Dwarves and Elves are the set''s clearest tribal signals, and they map directly onto the Red-White and Blue-Green archetypes WotC printed around them — the affinity map below shows both tribes sitting almost entirely on their two anchor colors, with very little bleed elsewhere. Goblins split mostly Black-Red, matching the Amass archetype; the remaining top creature types are broad enough that they don''t point at a single color pair the way Dwarf and Elf do.'
blockAloneConclusion: 'Taken together, the strongest signals in this set are: build around Graveyard/Mill or Draw payoffs first (the two cross-card synergies most likely to actually show up together), lean on Dwarf or Elf tribal support if you''re already in Red-White or Blue-Green, and watch for The Sackville-Bagginses and Belladonna Took specifically — both do real work across several of these themes at once.'
mechanicsIntro: 'Amass and Equip are the two mechanics most entangled with this set''s top synergy words, ahead of Flying and Treasure — a sign the set wants you engaging with combat math and board-state triggers, not just stapling keywords onto vanilla bodies.'
breadByColorIntro: 'Green and Red carry the highest average B.R.E.A.D. overall score, driven almost entirely by Bomb — big, efficient bodies are this set''s calling card in those two colors. Black leads on Removal, exactly where you''d expect it to, while White and Blue trail on raw power but hold up better on Diversity (card advantage, lifegain, fixing).'
conclusionText: 'If you''re picking a lane blind, Blue-Green Elves is the strongest overall pick, not Black-Green — it tops the full mana-combo ranking at an 87.3 final score, carried by the highest synergy density of any pair (81.6%, spread across Draw Matters, Landfall, Artifacts/Treasure, and Graveyard/Mill). The catch is real, though: its "wellSupported" verdict only holds with rares and mythics in the mix — strip those out and it drops to themeThin, so weight its handful of payoffs (Thranduil, Sindarin Liege; Thranduil''s Company) higher than their raw stats alone suggest. Black-Green still carries the set''s best average bomb strength, anchored by Large Bear and Chief Warg''s Company, but its own named archetype — Ferocious "Wolves" — is underprinted (six support cards, none rare or mythic); build around Trample instead if you land there. Black-Red Goblins is the one archetype that doesn''t need luck to function: it''s the only pair that keeps its plentifulButWeak verdict fully intact on commons alone, which makes it the safest fallback if Blue-Green gets cut early. Colorless fixing runs deep in this set — 13 cards, a 98.8% chance to open at least one across a 6-pack pool, led by Giant''s Boulder — so a black splash is a reasonable ask rather than a gamble; if Thranduil, the Elvenking specifically lands in your pool, that splash turns Elves from good into the pool''s best deck, since Thranduil doubles as a top enabler for the set''s #1 cross-card synergy, Graveyard/Mill, on top of its own card draw. White-Red Dwarves and White-Blue Humans both come back playable but unremarkable — treat them as what''s left if the other three colors don''t come together, not as a first choice.'
